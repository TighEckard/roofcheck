Commercial roof maintenance checklist

Most commercial roof leaks start small. A simple routine catches them early and helps protect your warranty.

Updated November 15, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Have a commercial roof inspected at least twice a year, typically in spring and fall, and after major storms. Keep drains and gutters clear, limit rooftop foot traffic, fix small seam and flashing problems quickly, and keep a log with photos. Many manufacturer warranties require documented maintenance.

Twice-a-year checklist

  • Clear drains, scuppers, gutters and downspouts of debris.
  • Look for ponding water that stays more than about two days after rain.
  • Check seams, edges and flashing at walls, curbs and penetrations.
  • Look for blisters, cracks, punctures, shrinkage or loose fasteners.
  • Check sealant at pitch pans, pipes and rooftop equipment.
  • Inspect walkway pads around HVAC units and service areas.
  • Look for interior signs: ceiling stains, wet insulation, musty smells.

After a storm

  • Check for blown-off edge metal, debris, punctures from hail or branches, and damaged rooftop units.
  • Photograph damage with dates before any cleanup.
  • Report damage to your insurer promptly. See filing a roof insurance claim.

Keep a roof file

Keep the installation date, contractor, roof system and warranty, plus every inspection report, repair invoice and photo. It makes warranty claims, insurance claims and budgeting much easier.

For HOAs and multifamily

Coordinate access with residents, plan work around weather and occupancy, and keep your reserve study up to date with current roof conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Who is responsible for the roof in a commercial lease?
It depends on the lease. Many leases make the landlord responsible for the roof structure and replacement, and some assign routine maintenance to the tenant. Check the lease before scheduling work.
How should an HOA plan for roof replacement?
Many associations use a reserve study to estimate when roofs will need replacing and how much to set aside. Regular inspections help keep that estimate accurate and catch problems early.

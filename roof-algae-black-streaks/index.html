Black streaks on your roof: algae, moss and lichen

Dark stains are common on shingle roofs in humid areas. Here's what they are and what to do.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Dark streaks on asphalt shingles are usually a type of algae, often called Gloeocapsa magma. It's mostly cosmetic. Moss and lichen are different: they hold moisture and can lift shingles, so they're worth removing. Clean gently with a roof-safe cleaner and low pressure, never a pressure washer. Algae-resistant shingles and zinc or copper strips near the ridge help prevent regrowth.

What you're looking at

  • Black or dark streaks running down the roof: algae. It grows on the shady, damp sides of the roof first.
  • Green, fuzzy clumps: moss. It traps water and can push shingle edges up.
  • Crusty, flat patches that cling tightly: lichen. Removing it by force can tear granules off.

Does it damage the roof?

Algae is mostly a looks problem, although dark streaks can make a roof absorb a bit more heat. Moss and heavy lichen are a real concern because they hold moisture against the shingles and under their edges, which can shorten the roof's life and lead to leaks.

How to clean it safely

  1. Hire a pro if the roof is steep, high or slippery. Wet, moss-covered roofs are dangerous.
  2. Use a cleaner made for roofs and follow the label. Shingle makers and roofing groups often suggest a diluted bleach solution or a commercial roof cleaner applied at low pressure.
  3. Rinse gently from the top down. Protect plants and keep runoff away from ponds and drains.
  4. Don't pressure wash or scrape. It strips granules, which protect shingles from the sun and can void warranties.
  5. Let dead moss loosen and fall off over time instead of prying it.

Prevention

  • Trim branches so the roof gets more sun and dries faster.
  • Keep gutters and valleys clear of leaves.
  • Zinc or copper strips near the ridge release metal ions in the rain that discourage growth.
  • When you replace the roof, choose algae-resistant (AR) shingles, which contain copper granules.

Frequently asked questions

Are black streaks on my roof mold?
They're usually algae, not mold. It's mostly cosmetic but can be cleaned and prevented.
Should I pressure wash my roof?
No. Pressure washing strips the protective granules from shingles and can void the warranty. Use a roof-safe cleaner at low pressure.
Does homeowners insurance cover roof moss?
Generally no. Moss and algae are treated as maintenance, not sudden damage.
Do I need a new roof because of moss?
Usually not, if it's caught early. Heavy moss that has lifted or rotted shingles may need repairs.

Do you need a permit to replace your roof?

In most of the U.S., a full roof replacement needs a permit. Here's how it works and why it matters.

Updated November 26, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Most cities and counties require a building permit for a full roof replacement, and many also require one for larger repairs. Small repairs like replacing a few shingles often don't need one. Rules are set locally, so check with your city or county building department. A licensed roofer usually pulls the permit and schedules the inspection for you.

Why the permit matters

  • An inspector checks the work against the building code, including underlayment, nailing, flashing and ventilation.
  • Unpermitted work can cause trouble when you sell or when you file an insurance claim.
  • Some insurers and lenders ask for proof of a permitted, inspected roof.
  • In hurricane-prone areas, a permitted roof may help you qualify for wind-mitigation discounts.

Who pulls it

Usually the roofing contractor. In many places only a licensed contractor (or the owner of a home they live in) can pull one. Be wary if a roofer asks you to pull the permit in your own name; that can make you responsible for code issues. Put "contractor obtains all permits" in the contract.

What it costs

Permit fees vary widely by city and are often based on the job's value. Ask for the fee to be listed on your quote.

Inspections

Some places inspect only at the end. Others also inspect mid-job, for example after the decking or underlayment goes on, especially in high-wind zones. Don't make final payment until the roof passes.

HOAs

If you have an HOA, you may also need its approval for material and color, separate from the city permit.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to repair my roof?
Small repairs often don't need one, but larger repairs or replacing a big section might. Your local building department sets the threshold.
Can I get in trouble for an unpermitted roof?
You may be asked to get a permit after the fact, open up the work for inspection, or fix code issues, and it can complicate a home sale or insurance claim.
How do I check if my roofer pulled a permit?
Many cities have online permit search by address. You can also ask the roofer for the permit number and call the building department.
Does a roof-over (second layer) need a permit?
Usually yes, and some places don't allow more than two layers or ban overlays entirely.

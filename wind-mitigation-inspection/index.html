Wind mitigation inspections: how they can lower your insurance

Common in Florida and other hurricane-prone areas, a wind mitigation inspection documents the features that help your home resist wind.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: A wind mitigation inspection documents how well your home's roof and openings resist hurricane winds. In Florida, insurers are required by law to offer discounts for qualifying wind-resistant features, and the inspection is usually recorded on the state's standard form (OIR-B1-1802). A new roof installed to current code, with the right nailing, secondary water barrier and roof-to-wall connections, can improve your results.

What the inspector checks

  • Roof covering and whether it meets current building code, with permit date.
  • Roof deck attachment: nail size and spacing holding the decking to the rafters.
  • Roof-to-wall attachment: toe nails, clips, single or double wraps (hurricane straps).
  • Roof shape: hip roofs generally score better than gable roofs.
  • Secondary water resistance: a sealed roof deck under the covering.
  • Opening protection: impact windows, doors and shutters.

Who does it

Licensed home inspectors, contractors, engineers and other professionals allowed by your state. Fees vary; ask before booking.

Timing with a new roof

If you're replacing your roof, ask the roofer about upgrades that affect the report, such as sealing the deck and re-nailing the decking to current code. Then get a new inspection after the permit passes and send it to your insurer.

Frequently asked questions

How much can a wind mitigation inspection save?
It depends on your insurer, home and features found. Some homeowners see meaningful savings on the windstorm portion of their premium; others see little change.
How long is a wind mitigation report valid?
In Florida, insurers generally accept the report for up to five years, as long as nothing material changes. Check with your insurer.
Do I need a wind mitigation inspection outside Florida?
Some insurers in other coastal states offer similar discounts, such as for roofs built to FORTIFIED standards. Ask your agent.
Does a new roof automatically lower my premium?
Not automatically. Send your insurer the permit and, where applicable, an updated wind mitigation report.

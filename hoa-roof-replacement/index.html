HOA and condo roof replacement: a guide for boards

Replacing roofs across a community is a big project. Here's how boards and managers keep it organized and fair.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Start with your governing documents to see whether the association or individual owners are responsible for roofs. Use a reserve study and a professional roof assessment to plan timing and budget. Then bid the work with one written scope, check licenses and insurance, and communicate the schedule clearly to owners. Many associations phase replacements over several years to match reserves.

1. Know who is responsible

CC&Rs and bylaws decide whether the association or each owner maintains and replaces roofs, and state law can affect this, especially for condominiums. If it's unclear, ask the association's attorney before bidding.

2. Plan with data

  • A reserve study estimates when roofs will need replacing and how much to set aside.
  • A roof condition assessment by a qualified roofer or consultant ranks buildings by need, with photos.
  • Phasing the worst roofs first can spread cost and avoid special assessments.

3. Bid it right

  • Write one scope: materials, colors approved by the architectural committee, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, cleanup and warranty.
  • Get at least three bids from licensed, insured roofers. Ask for references from other communities.
  • Confirm who pulls permits and how damage to landscaping, cars and gutters is handled.

4. Communicate with owners

Send schedules and notices well ahead: which buildings, which days, parking changes, noise hours, and who to call with concerns. Ask owners to move vehicles and fragile items from walls during tear-off.

5. Insurance and storms

After hail or wind, check the master policy and deductibles before signing anything. Be wary of contractors going door-to-door asking individual owners to sign. See roofing scams and insurance claims.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays for roof replacement in an HOA?
It depends on the governing documents and state law. In many condominiums the association pays from reserves; in many single-family HOAs owners pay.
Does an HOA need multiple roofing bids?
Many boards get at least three, and some governing documents or state laws require competitive bidding for large projects.
Can an HOA require a specific roof color or material?
Usually, through architectural guidelines, though some states limit restrictions on certain materials like solar or impact-resistant shingles.
How long does a community roof project take?
Each building often takes a few days, weather permitting. A whole community may be phased over weeks or years.

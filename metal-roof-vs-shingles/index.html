Metal roof vs. asphalt shingles: which is better?

The two most common choices for a new roof on a U.S. home, compared side by side.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Asphalt shingles cost less up front and are what most U.S. homes have. Metal roofing usually costs more to install but lasts much longer, often 40 years or more versus roughly 15 to 30 for shingles, and it handles wind and wildfire embers well. If you plan to stay in the home a long time, metal can cost less over the roof's life. If you're selling in a few years or working to a tight budget, architectural shingles are usually the better value.

When shingles make more sense

  • You expect to sell within about 10 years.
  • Your budget is fixed and the roof needs replacing now.
  • Your HOA limits roof materials or colors.

When metal makes more sense

  • You plan to stay 20+ years and want one roof for the long haul.
  • You're in wildfire country or a high-wind coastal area.
  • You get heavy snow and want it to shed easily (add snow guards over doors and walkways).

Hail and insurance

In hail country, ask your insurer how they treat cosmetic dents on metal. Some policies exclude cosmetic damage to metal roofs, which can matter after a big storm. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles are the other option many hail-belt homeowners compare. See Class 4 shingles.

Get both priced

Prices vary a lot by region and roof shape, so ask a roofer to quote both a shingle and a metal option on the same visit. Compare the full scope: tear-off, decking repair, underlayment, flashing, ventilation and warranty.

Frequently asked questions

Is a metal roof louder than shingles?
Usually not by much. Over solid roof decking with underlayment, most homeowners say rain on a metal roof isn't noticeably louder. Metal over open framing, like on a barn, is louder.
Does a metal roof attract lightning?
No. Metal doesn't make a strike more likely. If lightning does hit, metal is noncombustible and spreads the energy.
Can metal go over existing shingles?
Sometimes, if local code allows it and the deck is sound. Many roofers still recommend a full tear-off so they can inspect and repair the decking.
Which adds more resale value?
A newer roof of either kind helps a sale. Metal can be a selling point in some markets, but buyers rarely pay back the full extra cost.

Roof warranties explained

Two different warranties cover a new roof. Here's what each one does and how to protect both.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: A new roof usually has two warranties. The manufacturer warranty covers defects in the shingles or other materials. The workmanship warranty comes from your roofer and covers installation mistakes, which cause most early roof problems. Get the workmanship warranty in writing, ask how many years it lasts, and keep your invoice and product details.

Manufacturer warranty

Covers defective materials. Many shingle warranties are prorated after an initial period, so coverage shrinks over time. Some manufacturers offer enhanced warranties, which can include some labor coverage, when a roof is installed by a credentialed contractor using a full system of their components. Read what's actually covered; "lifetime" often has conditions.

Workmanship warranty

Covers installation errors such as poor flashing, wrong nailing or bad ventilation. Length varies a lot between roofers. It's only worth something if the company is still in business, so ask how long they've operated locally.

What can void a warranty

  • Installing shingles in a way the manufacturer doesn't allow.
  • Poor attic ventilation.
  • Later work by another contractor on the same area.
  • Not registering the warranty when registration is required.

Questions to ask before signing

  1. How long is your workmanship warranty, and is it in the contract?
  2. Which manufacturer warranty will this roof get, and will you register it?
  3. Is the warranty transferable if I sell?
  4. What do I need to do to keep it valid?

Frequently asked questions

What does a lifetime roof warranty mean?
Usually that the manufacturer covers material defects for as long as you own the home, often prorated after the first years. It rarely covers labor or storm damage unless stated.
Does a roof warranty cover storm damage?
Generally no. Storm damage is usually a homeowner's insurance matter. Some shingles have separate wind ratings or limited wind warranties.
Is a roof warranty transferable?
Many manufacturer warranties can transfer once, sometimes with a notice or fee. Check the terms, as this can help when you sell.
Who do I call with a warranty problem?
Start with your roofer for leaks or installation issues. For a suspected material defect, the manufacturer will usually want photos, the product details and proof of purchase.

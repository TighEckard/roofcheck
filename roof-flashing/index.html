Roof flashing: what it is and why it leaks

Many roof leaks start not in the shingles but at the flashing where the roof meets something else.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Flashing is thin metal (or sometimes rubber) that seals the joints where a roof meets a wall, chimney, skylight, vent pipe or valley. Those joints are where water most often gets in. Rusted, loose or poorly sealed flashing is one of the most common causes of leaks, and it can often be repaired without replacing the whole roof.

Types of flashing

  • Step flashing: L-shaped pieces woven in with each course of shingles where a roof meets a side wall.
  • Counter flashing: set into a chimney or wall and lapped over the step flashing.
  • Valley flashing: lines the valley where two roof slopes meet.
  • Drip edge: metal along the eaves and rakes that guides water into the gutter and protects the deck edge.
  • Pipe boots: collars around plumbing vents. Rubber boots crack in the sun over time.
  • Skylight and chimney kits: pre-formed flashing for those openings.

Signs of failing flashing

  • Stains on the ceiling or wall near a chimney, skylight or where a roof meets a wall.
  • Rust, gaps, or flashing that has pulled away.
  • Caulk or roofing cement smeared over flashing as a quick fix, which often fails again.
  • Cracked rubber around vent pipes.

Repair or replace?

If the roof is in good shape, a roofer can usually replace the failing flashing or pipe boot on its own. If the roof is near the end of its life, it often makes sense to redo the flashing as part of a full replacement. Ask whether your quote includes new flashing rather than reusing old pieces. See repair or replace.

Questions for your roofer

  • Will you install new step and counter flashing, or reuse the old?
  • Is drip edge included? Many codes now require it.
  • What material are the pipe boots, and how long do they last?

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common cause of roof leaks?
Failing flashing and cracked pipe boots are among the most common. Leaks tend to start where the roof meets something else.
Can flashing be repaired without a new roof?
Often yes. A roofer can replace flashing or a pipe boot and weave in matching shingles if the rest of the roof is sound.
Should old flashing be reused on a new roof?
Many roofers replace it, especially step flashing and drip edge, because old flashing can be bent or corroded. Ask what your quote includes.
Is caulk enough to fix flashing?
Caulk or roofing cement is a short-term patch. A lasting fix is properly installed flashing.

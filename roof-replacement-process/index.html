What to expect during a roof replacement

From the dumpster arriving to the final walkthrough, here's how a typical roof replacement goes.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Most roof replacements on a typical single-family home take one to three days once work starts. The crew tears off the old roof, repairs damaged decking, installs underlayment, flashing and the new roof covering, then cleans up with magnets for nails. Larger, steeper or more complex roofs, and metal, tile or slate, can take longer.

Before the job

  • Sign a written contract with the scope, materials, price, payment schedule, permit and warranty.
  • Confirm the permit and the start date.
  • Move cars, patio furniture and grills away from the house.
  • Take down wall hangings on top-floor walls; hammering can shake them.
  • Cover items in the attic to catch dust.
  • Plan for pets and kids; it's loud.

Day by day

  1. Setup: dumpster or trailer, tarps over plants and siding.
  2. Tear-off: old shingles and underlayment come off down to the decking.
  3. Decking: rotted or soft boards are replaced. Contracts often price this per sheet, so ask in advance.
  4. Underlayment and ice/water shield in valleys, eaves and around penetrations, as code requires.
  5. Flashing, drip edge and vents.
  6. New roof covering.
  7. Ridge cap and final details.
  8. Cleanup: magnetic sweep for nails, debris hauled away.

Final walkthrough

Walk the property with the crew lead. Ask for photos of the decking and underlayment stages, check gutters and the yard for nails, confirm the permit passed inspection, and get warranty paperwork before final payment. See roof warranties.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a roof replacement take?
One to three days of work is typical for an average single-family home with asphalt shingles. Weather, roof size, steepness and material can extend that.
Do I need to be home during a roof replacement?
Not usually, but be reachable in case the crew finds rotten decking or needs a decision.
What if they find rotten wood?
The roofer replaces damaged decking, usually at a price per sheet agreed in the contract. Ask to see photos.
Will my gutters be removed?
Usually not, though some may be loosened to install drip edge. Ask whether gutter work is included.

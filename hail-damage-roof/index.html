How to tell if hail damaged your roof

Hail damage is easy to miss from the ground. Here are the signs to look for after a storm and what to do next.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Signs to look for from the ground

  • Dents in gutters, downspouts, vents and air-conditioning units.
  • Granules from shingles collecting in gutters or at the bottom of downspouts.
  • Damaged siding, window screens or outdoor furniture.
  • Shingle pieces in the yard.

What hail does to shingles

Hail can leave dark spots where granules were knocked off, soft "bruises" that feel like a bruised apple, and cracks or punctures. Damage that looks minor can still shorten a roof's life. Please don't climb on the roof yourself. A roofer can inspect safely and photograph what they find.

After a hailstorm

  1. Photograph damage you can see from the ground, along with the hail itself if you can, with dates.
  2. Get a roofer's inspection and written findings.
  3. Report the damage to your insurer and ask about your deductible. Some states and policies have separate wind and hail deductibles.
  4. Be careful with storm chasers knocking on doors. Check the license and local address of anyone you hire. See how to choose a roofer.

Frequently asked questions

Can hail damage a roof without causing a leak?
Yes. Hail can bruise shingles and knock off the protective granules, which shortens the roof's life and can lead to leaks later.
How long do I have to file a hail claim?
It depends on your policy and state. Many policies set a deadline for reporting damage, so check yours and report promptly.
What are impact-resistant shingles?
Shingles tested to resist hail impacts, often labeled Class 4. They cost more, but some insurers in hail-prone states offer premium discounts for them.

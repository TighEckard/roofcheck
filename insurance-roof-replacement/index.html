Does homeowners insurance cover roof replacement?

Insurance can pay for a new roof in some cases, but not all. Here's how it usually works, in plain English.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

This is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Your policy and your insurer decide what's covered.

What's usually covered

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental damage from covered events, such as wind, hail, fire or a falling tree. If a storm damages your roof, your policy may pay to repair or replace it, minus your deductible.

What usually isn't

  • Wear and tear from age.
  • Damage from lack of maintenance, like long-term leaks that weren't fixed.
  • Problems from poor installation.
  • Events your policy excludes, such as flood (which needs separate flood insurance).

Things that affect your payout

  • Your deductible. Some policies, especially in coastal and hail-prone states, have separate percentage deductibles for wind, hail or hurricanes.
  • Replacement cost or actual cash value. Actual cash value pays less because it subtracts depreciation for the roof's age.
  • Roof age. Some insurers limit coverage on older roofs or ask about roof age before renewing a policy.

How a roof claim usually works

  1. Document the damage with dated photos.
  2. Report it to your insurer promptly.
  3. An adjuster inspects the roof.
  4. Get a roofer's written estimate so you can compare it with the adjuster's.
  5. If you disagree with the decision, ask your insurer how to dispute it.

Frequently asked questions

Will insurance replace an old roof?
Usually only if a covered event, like a storm, damaged it. Normal wear and tear from age generally isn't covered. Some policies pay less on older roofs, so read yours.
What's the difference between replacement cost and actual cash value?
Replacement cost coverage pays to replace the roof with similar materials, minus your deductible. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation for the roof's age, so it pays less.
Should a roofer handle my insurance claim?
A roofer can document damage and give you an estimate, but your claim is between you and your insurer. Be careful about signing anything that hands over your insurance benefits before you understand it, and never agree to have your deductible waived.

Ice dams: what causes them and how to prevent them

If you see a thick ridge of ice and icicles at the edge of your roof, here's what's happening and what to do.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Ice dams form when heat escaping from the house melts snow on the upper roof, and the meltwater refreezes at the colder roof edge. Water then backs up under the shingles and can leak into ceilings and walls. The long-term fix is to keep the roof deck cold with better attic air sealing, insulation and ventilation, plus ice and water shield along the eaves when the roof is replaced.

Signs of an ice dam

  • A thick ridge of ice along the eaves and heavy icicles.
  • Water stains or dripping on ceilings or exterior walls during a thaw.
  • Ice forming inside the soffits or gutters.

What to do now (safely)

  • Don't climb on an icy roof or chip at ice with tools. It's dangerous and damages shingles.
  • A roof rake used from the ground can pull snow off the lower few feet of the roof.
  • Professional ice dam removal with low-pressure steam is safer for the roof than hammers or chisels.
  • Catch drips inside and photograph any damage for insurance.

Long-term fixes

  1. Air-seal the attic floor: gaps around light fixtures, pipes and the attic hatch let warm air in.
  2. Add insulation to recommended levels for your climate.
  3. Balance ventilation with intake at the soffits and exhaust at the ridge.
  4. Ice and water shield at the eaves when you replace the roof; many cold-climate building codes require it.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover ice dam damage?
Many policies cover sudden water damage inside the home from an ice dam, but often not the ice removal itself or long-term maintenance problems. Check your policy and call your agent.
Do gutters cause ice dams?
Not by themselves. Clogged gutters can make ice buildup worse, but the main cause is heat loss melting snow higher on the roof.
Do heated roof cables work?
They can keep channels open in problem areas, but they use electricity and don't fix the heat loss behind the problem.
Will a new roof stop ice dams?
A new roof with ice and water shield at the eaves limits leaks, but ice dams can still form unless attic insulation and ventilation are fixed.

Roof and attic ventilation explained

Ventilation is easy to overlook, but it affects shingle life, ice dams, moisture and comfort.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Most roofs over an attic need balanced ventilation: cool air comes in low at the soffits (intake) and warm, moist air leaves high at the ridge (exhaust). Poor ventilation can trap heat and moisture, shorten shingle life, cause mold and contribute to ice dams. Shingle manufacturers commonly require proper ventilation for full warranty coverage, so ask your roofer to check it during any replacement.

Intake and exhaust

  • Intake: soffit vents or edge vents near the eaves.
  • Exhaust: ridge vents, box (static) vents, or powered fans near the top.
  • Mixing different exhaust types on the same attic can short-circuit airflow; roofers usually pick one.

Signs of poor ventilation

  • Very hot attic in summer, high cooling bills.
  • Frost or condensation on the underside of the roof deck in winter.
  • Mold, musty smell or rusty nails in the attic.
  • Ice dams in winter.
  • Shingles aging faster on one section of roof.

What to ask during a replacement

  1. Is my current ventilation adequate for the attic size?
  2. Are the soffit vents blocked by insulation?
  3. What exhaust will you install, and will it meet the shingle maker's requirements?

Note: some homes use an unvented ("conditioned") attic with insulation under the roof deck. That's a different design, so don't add vents without professional advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much attic ventilation do I need?
A common rule in building codes is 1 square foot of net free vent area per 150 square feet of attic floor, or 1 per 300 in some cases with balanced intake and exhaust. Your roofer or local code will confirm.
Are ridge vents better than box vents?
Ridge vents spread exhaust evenly along the peak and are common on new roofs. Box vents work too. The key is balance with intake at the eaves.
Can too much ventilation be a problem?
Mixing exhaust types or having too little intake can pull air the wrong way, including weather. Balance matters more than quantity.
Does ventilation affect my shingle warranty?
Often yes. Many shingle manufacturers require adequate ventilation for full warranty coverage.

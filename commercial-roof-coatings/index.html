Commercial roof coatings: restore or replace?

A coating can add years to a commercial roof for less than a replacement, but only if the roof underneath is in good shape.

Updated November 26, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: A roof coating (silicone, acrylic or polyurethane) can extend the life of a commercial roof that is still mostly dry and sound, usually for less than a full replacement and with less disruption. It's not a fix for a roof with widespread wet insulation, failing seams everywhere or structural problems. A roofer should check for trapped moisture before recommending one.

When a coating can make sense

  • The roof is aging but still watertight, with only isolated problems that can be repaired first.
  • A moisture survey or core cuts show the insulation is mostly dry.
  • You want a reflective surface to cut cooling loads.
  • You want to delay a full tear-off for budget or tenant reasons.

When replacement is the better call

  • Large areas of wet insulation.
  • Widespread seam failure, blistering or shrinkage.
  • Deck damage or structural sagging.
  • The roof already has the maximum number of layers allowed by code.

Main coating types

  • Silicone: handles ponding water and UV well. Recoating later usually requires silicone again.
  • Acrylic: water-based, reflective and usually less expensive. Best where water drains well.
  • Polyurethane and SPF: tough coatings, and spray polyurethane foam can add insulation and fix low spots.

Questions to ask

  1. How did you check the roof for trapped moisture?
  2. What repairs will be made before coating, including seams, flashing and drains?
  3. What coating thickness will be applied, and how will you verify it?
  4. What warranty comes with the system, and what maintenance does it require?

Frequently asked questions

Can you put a coating on a leaking roof?
Coatings aren't a fix for an active leak or a wet roof. Leaks need to be repaired and wet insulation replaced first. A coating then goes over a sound, dry roof.
Silicone or acrylic coating?
Silicone holds up well to ponding water and UV. Acrylic is usually less expensive and very reflective but is generally not recommended where water ponds. The existing roof type and local climate decide which is a better fit.
Does a roof coating qualify as a new roof?
Usually not. It's a restoration that extends the life of the existing roof. Some coating systems come with their own manufacturer warranty, and the terms vary.

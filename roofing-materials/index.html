Roofing materials compared: asphalt, metal, tile and slate

The right roof depends on your climate, budget, home style and how long you plan to stay. Here's how the main options compare.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Asphalt shingles are the cheapest and most common choice and typically last 15 to 30 years. Metal costs more but often lasts 40 to 70 years and handles wind, snow and embers well. Concrete and clay tile suit hot, sunny and hurricane-prone areas and can last 50 years or more. Slate lasts the longest but is the most expensive and heaviest.

How climate changes the choice

  • Hail country (Texas to the Dakotas and the Front Range): impact-resistant shingles or metal. See hail damage.
  • Hurricane coasts: high-wind-rated shingles, tile or standing-seam metal installed to local wind codes, plus a sealed roof deck.
  • Snow and ice: any good material, plus an ice-and-water barrier at the eaves and proper attic ventilation to prevent ice dams.
  • Desert heat: tile or reflective "cool roof" materials to cut heat gain.
  • Wildfire areas: Class A fire-rated roofing, such as asphalt, metal or tile, with ember-resistant vents.
  • Wet Pacific Northwest: moss-resistant shingles or metal, with careful flashing.

Questions to ask about any material

  1. What wind and impact ratings does this product have, and what does my local code require?
  2. What does the manufacturer's warranty cover, and does it require a certified installer?
  3. What underlayment, flashing and ventilation are included in the quote?
  4. Can my roof structure carry the weight? (Important for tile and slate.)
  5. Are there HOA or historic-district rules on color or material?

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common roofing material in the U.S.?
Asphalt shingles. They cover most American homes because they're affordable, widely available and easy to repair.
Is a metal roof worth the extra cost?
It can be if you plan to stay in the home a long time. Metal usually costs more up front but often lasts two to three times as long as asphalt shingles and handles wind, snow and wildfire embers well.
What roof lasts the longest?
Natural slate and clay tile can last a century or more when installed and maintained well. Their weight and cost limit where they're used.
Can I put a new roof over my old shingles?
Some codes allow one extra layer of asphalt shingles over an existing layer, but many roofers recommend a full tear-off so they can inspect and repair the decking. Your local building code and the shingle manufacturer's warranty terms decide what's allowed.

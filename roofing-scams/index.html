Roofing scams and red flags: how to protect yourself

Most roofers are honest, but storms bring out a few who aren't. These are the scams homeowners run into most, and how to avoid them.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: The biggest roofing red flags are a roofer who knocks on your door right after a storm, pushes you to sign the same day, offers to pay or waive your insurance deductible, wants a large cash payment up front, or can't show a license (where your state requires one) and proof of insurance. Check any roofer before you sign, and never let anyone pressure you into a decision on the spot.

The most common roofing scams

1. The door-knock after a storm

After hail or high winds, crews go door to door offering free inspections. Some are reputable local companies, but some are out-of-town crews that will be gone when the warranty matters. Ask for a local address and look the company up before you let anyone on your roof.

2. "We'll cover your deductible"

An offer to pay, rebate or waive your deductible sounds generous, but it means the roofer is either inflating the claim or cutting corners on the work. It's a form of insurance fraud, and states including Texas and Florida specifically prohibit it.

3. Damage that wasn't there before

Dishonest inspectors have been caught creating "hail hits" or tearing shingles to make damage look worse. If someone goes up on your roof, ask for clear, dated photos, and consider getting a second opinion before filing a claim.

4. The contingency agreement that's really a contract

Some roofers ask you to sign a form "so we can talk to your insurance company." Read it carefully. Some of these forms commit you to hiring that roofer if the claim is approved. Several states regulate these agreements and give you a short window to cancel, so ask what you're signing and get a copy.

5. Large cash deposits

Paying most of the job in cash before work starts leaves you with little leverage if the crew never shows up. Pay by check or card, keep receipts, and tie payments to progress, like delivery of materials and completion.

6. The too-good-to-be-true price

A bid far below the others can mean skipped underlayment, reused flashing, no permit or uninsured workers. Compare itemized quotes line by line so you know what each one includes.

Red flags checklist

  • Pressure to sign today, or "this price is only good right now".
  • No physical local address, or a phone number that goes straight to voicemail.
  • Can't or won't show a license (where required) and current certificates of liability and workers' comp insurance.
  • Offers to handle your claim "for free" in exchange for a signature.
  • Asks you to sign a blank or incomplete contract.
  • Won't pull a building permit, or asks you to pull it yourself.
  • Only accepts cash.

How to check a roofer in 10 minutes

  1. Look up the license or registration on your state or city website. Our state pages explain each state's rules.
  2. Ask for certificates of insurance and call the insurer to confirm they're current.
  3. Search the company name with words like "complaint" or "scam", and check your state attorney general's site.
  4. Ask for two or three local references from the past year.
  5. Get the warranty, the scope of work and the payment schedule in writing.

If you think you've been scammed

Stop payment if you can, keep every document and photo, and report it to your state attorney general's consumer protection office and your insurer. The Federal Trade Commission also takes reports at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Frequently asked questions

What is a storm chaser roofer?
A storm chaser is a roofing crew, often from out of state, that follows major hail and wind storms and goes door to door looking for insurance-paid jobs. Some are legitimate, but many are hard to reach once the job is done, which matters when a warranty or leak problem comes up.
Can a roofer pay or waive my insurance deductible?
No. Your deductible is your share of the claim. Offering to cover or waive it is a form of insurance fraud, and several states, including Texas and Florida, specifically prohibit contractors from offering it.
Should I sign a contract with a roofer on the day of the inspection?
You don't have to, and it's usually better not to. Get at least two written quotes, check the contractor's license and insurance, and read any contract or insurance agreement before signing.
How much should a roofing deposit be?
It varies by company and by state, and some states cap deposits on home improvement contracts. Be cautious of anyone who wants a large share of the price in cash before materials arrive, and pay by a traceable method.

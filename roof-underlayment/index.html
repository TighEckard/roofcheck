Roof underlayment: felt vs. synthetic, and why it matters

Underlayment is the layer between the roof deck and the shingles or metal. It's your backup when water gets past the top layer.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Underlayment is a water-resistant layer installed over the roof deck before shingles, metal or tile go on. Most new residential roofs today use synthetic underlayment, which is lighter, stronger and less prone to tearing than traditional asphalt felt. Self-adhering ice-and-water membrane is added at eaves, valleys and around openings, especially where ice dams or wind-driven rain are a risk.

The main types

TypeWhat it isWhere it's used
Asphalt felt (#15 or #30)Paper or fiberglass mat soaked in asphaltTraditional; lower cost; can wrinkle when wet
SyntheticWoven or spun polymer sheetsMost common on new roofs; lighter and tear-resistant
Self-adhering membrane (ice and water shield)Rubberized asphalt with a sticky back that seals around nailsEaves, valleys, around chimneys and skylights; whole-roof in some high-wind and coastal areas

Why it matters

  • It keeps the deck dry during installation.
  • It's a second barrier if shingles blow off or water is driven under them.
  • Some shingle warranties require specific underlayment products.
  • Codes in cold climates often require ice barrier at the eaves, and some coastal areas encourage or require a sealed roof deck.

What to look for in a quote

  • The underlayment type and brand, not just "underlayment".
  • Where ice-and-water membrane will go (eaves, valleys, around openings, or the whole roof).
  • Whether it meets the shingle maker's requirements for the warranty you're being sold.

Frequently asked questions

Is synthetic underlayment better than felt?
For most new roofs, yes. It's lighter, stronger and resists tearing and wrinkling. Felt is cheaper and still meets code in many places.
What is ice and water shield?
A self-adhering rubberized membrane that seals around nails. It goes at eaves, valleys and openings where water is most likely to back up.
Do I need ice and water shield on the whole roof?
Not usually. It's commonly required at the eaves in cold climates. In some high-wind or coastal areas, a fully sealed deck is recommended.
Can underlayment be replaced without new shingles?
No. It sits under the shingles, so replacing it means removing the roof covering.

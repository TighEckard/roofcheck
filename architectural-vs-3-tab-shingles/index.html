Architectural vs. 3-tab shingles: what's the difference?

The two main types of asphalt shingles, and why most roofers now quote architectural.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: 3-tab shingles are thin, flat, single-layer shingles with a uniform look. Architectural (also called dimensional or laminated) shingles are made of two or more bonded layers, so they're thicker, have a textured look, usually carry higher wind ratings and longer warranties, and cost somewhat more. Most new residential roofs in the U.S. now use architectural shingles, and some manufacturers have reduced or discontinued 3-tab lines.

When 3-tab still makes sense

  • Repairing an existing 3-tab roof so patches match.
  • Sheds, garages or rentals where the lowest price matters most.

Beyond architectural

"Designer" or luxury shingles are heavier still and mimic slate or shake. Class 4 impact-resistant versions are available in many architectural lines. See Class 4 shingles and all roofing materials.

Frequently asked questions

Are architectural shingles worth the extra cost?
For most homeowners, yes: better wind resistance, a longer warranty and better curb appeal for a modest price difference on a full roof.
Can you mix 3-tab and architectural shingles?
On separate roof sections, sometimes, but they look different side by side. For repairs, roofers try to match the existing type.
How can I tell which shingles I have?
3-tab shingles show evenly spaced cutouts and a flat look. Architectural shingles look layered and uneven, with no regular tabs.
Do architectural shingles need special installation?
They follow the manufacturer's nailing pattern like any shingle; the key is a crew that installs to spec so the warranty applies.

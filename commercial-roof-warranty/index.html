Commercial roof warranties: manufacturer vs. contractor

A commercial roof warranty is only as useful as its fine print. Here's what the common types cover.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Commercial roofs usually carry two kinds of warranty. A manufacturer warranty covers materials and, in the stronger "system" or "no dollar limit" (NDL) versions, the cost of repairing leaks caused by materials or installation, for a set number of years. A contractor workmanship warranty covers installation errors, often for a shorter period. Read both, since exclusions and maintenance requirements decide whether they pay.

What commonly voids or limits coverage

  • Repairs or new penetrations by unauthorized contractors.
  • Skipped maintenance, like clogged drains or debris left on the roof.
  • Damage from foot traffic, other trades or rooftop equipment.
  • Severe weather beyond stated wind speeds; check the wind rating.
  • Not registering the warranty or paying the issuing fee.

Tips before you sign

  • Ask for a sample warranty document from the manufacturer, not a brochure.
  • Confirm the term, wind speed rating and whether it transfers if you sell.
  • Keep inspection and maintenance records on file.

Frequently asked questions

What is an NDL roof warranty?
No Dollar Limit: a manufacturer system warranty that pays for covered leak repairs without a cap on the dollar amount, for its term.
Who issues a commercial roof warranty?
The membrane manufacturer issues the material or system warranty; the installing contractor issues the workmanship warranty.
Does maintenance affect my roof warranty?
Often yes. Many warranties require reasonable maintenance and may exclude damage from clogged drains or debris.
Can I transfer a commercial roof warranty?
Some are transferable with notice and a fee. Check the terms before you sell.

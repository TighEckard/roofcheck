How to pay for a new roof: financing options

A new roof is a big expense. Here are the common ways homeowners pay for one, and what to watch out for.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Homeowners usually pay for a new roof with savings, an insurance claim (if the damage is from a covered event like hail or wind), contractor-arranged financing, a personal loan, or a home equity loan or line of credit. Compare the total cost of borrowing, not just the monthly payment, and be careful with "0% interest" offers that charge deferred interest if not paid off in time.

Common options

OptionGood forWatch out for
Insurance claimSudden storm or hail damageDeductible; age or wear isn't covered
Contractor financingConvenience; promo ratesDeferred interest; fees; high rates after promo
Personal loanNo home collateralHigher rates for weaker credit
Home equity loan / HELOCLower rates for homeowners with equityYour home secures the loan; closing costs

Assistance programs

Some homeowners qualify for help. Low-income and older homeowners can ask their local housing agency, Area Agency on Aging, or USDA Rural Development (for rural homes) about repair grants or loans. Some states also offer grants to strengthen roofs against hurricanes.

Tips

  1. Get your quotes first, then compare financing separately.
  2. Read the fine print on any promotional rate.
  3. Never sign financing paperwork you don't understand on the spot.

Frequently asked questions

Can I finance a new roof with bad credit?
Possibly, through some contractor programs or lenders that work with lower credit scores, but expect higher rates. Compare total cost carefully.
Is a home equity loan a good way to pay for a roof?
It often has lower rates than unsecured loans, but your home is collateral. It suits homeowners with equity and steady income.
Are there grants for roof replacement?
Some local, state and federal programs help eligible homeowners, often based on income, age, disability or rural location. Availability varies by area.
Does insurance pay for an old roof?
Insurance generally pays for sudden damage from covered events, not wear and age. Older roofs may be paid at actual cash value.

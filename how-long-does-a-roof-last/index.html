How long does a roof last?

Every roof wears out eventually. Here's roughly how long common roofing materials last and what affects their lifespan.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

These are general ranges. Climate, installation quality, ventilation and maintenance can move the real number a lot in either direction.

MaterialTypical lifespan
Asphalt shinglesRoughly 15 to 30 years, with architectural shingles at the longer end
MetalOften 40 years or more
Wood shakesRoughly 20 to 40 years with upkeep
Clay or concrete tile50 years or more for the tiles, though the underlayment may need replacing sooner
SlateOften 75 years or more
Flat roofs (TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen)Roughly 15 to 30 years depending on the system

What shortens a roof's life

  • Hot, sunny climates and strong UV.
  • Hail, wind and storms.
  • Poor attic ventilation that traps heat and moisture.
  • Overhanging trees, clogged gutters and moss.
  • Poor installation.

How to make it last

  • Keep gutters clean and trim back branches.
  • Have the roof checked after major storms.
  • Fix small problems like loose flashing or cracked pipe boots early.
  • Make sure the attic is properly ventilated and insulated.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a roof wear out faster?
Intense sun and heat, poor attic ventilation, storms, trees rubbing or dropping debris, and poor installation all shorten a roof's life.
Does a warranty tell me how long my roof will last?
Not exactly. Manufacturer warranties cover material defects and often have conditions. Ask your roofer to explain what's covered and for how long.

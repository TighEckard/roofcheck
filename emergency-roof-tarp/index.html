Emergency roof tarping after storm damage

After a storm opens up your roof, a tarp buys time and limits water damage until repairs.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: If storm damage lets water in, an emergency tarp can prevent much bigger interior damage. Most homeowner policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and many cover the cost of emergency tarping as part of a covered claim. Climbing a damaged, wet roof is dangerous, so hire a roofer or emergency service when you can. Keep photos and receipts.

Right after the storm

  1. Stay safe. Stay off the roof in wind, rain or darkness, and watch for downed power lines.
  2. Photograph damage outside and inside before anything is moved.
  3. Catch water inside with buckets and move belongings.
  4. Call a roofer or tarping service, and call your insurer to start a claim.

How a proper tarp is installed

  • A heavy-duty tarp extending well past the damaged area, ideally over the ridge so water runs off.
  • Fastened with wood battens screwed or nailed through the tarp's edges, not just weighed down with bricks.
  • Edges sealed so wind can't get under it.

How long a tarp lasts

A tarp is temporary. Sun and wind wear it down, so schedule permanent repairs as soon as possible. After hurricanes, some areas have free federal or local temporary roofing programs; check official announcements.

Frequently asked questions

Does insurance pay for an emergency roof tarp?
Often, as part of a covered claim, because policies expect you to prevent further damage. Keep the invoice and photos and check your policy.
Can I tarp my roof myself?
It's possible on a low, dry, safe roof, but falls are a real risk. A roofer or tarping service is safer, especially on steep or wet roofs.
How long can a roof tarp stay on?
It's meant to be temporary. Tarps degrade in sun and wind, so plan permanent repairs promptly.
Should I sign with the company that tarps my roof?
You can hire a company to tarp without signing a full repair contract. Read anything carefully before signing, especially assignment-of-benefits forms.

Common roof problems in Texas

  • Hailstorms that bruise and crack shingles, sometimes without an obvious leak.
  • Summer thunderstorms and humidity that speed up shingle aging and algae streaks.
  • Hurricane and tropical-storm winds that lift shingles and drive rain under flashing.
  • Intense sun and heat that dry out and crack shingles and underlayment.

Impact-resistant (often called Class 4) shingles and metal roofing are popular here. Some insurers offer premium discounts for impact-rated roofs, so ask your agent.

Tip: Hail damage isn't always visible from the ground. If a storm hit your neighborhood, a roofer's inspection with photos can show what's there before you call your insurer.

Checking a roofer's license in Texas

Texas has no statewide license for roofers, so anyone can call themselves a roofer. That makes references, a local address, proof of insurance and a written contract especially important. Some Texas cities require roofers to register locally before pulling a permit.

Ask every contractor for their license or registration number and check it before you sign. Our roofer checklist covers the rest.

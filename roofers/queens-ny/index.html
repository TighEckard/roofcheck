Roofing in Queens: what to know

Queens has more detached and semi-detached homes than most of New York City, so pitched shingle roofs are common alongside flat-roofed attached houses. Coastal areas like the Rockaways face nor'easters and were badly damaged by Hurricane Sandy in 2012.

What wears out roofs around Queens

  • Nor'easters and the occasional tropical storm bringing wind-driven rain.
  • Salt air near the water that corrodes metal flashing, fasteners and vents.
  • Snow loads and ice dams that push meltwater under shingles at the eaves.

Best roofing materials for Queens

Around Queens, roofers often recommend well-sealed flashing and wind-rated architectural shingles, corrosion-resistant flashing and fasteners, and an ice-and-water barrier along the eaves plus good attic insulation and ventilation.

Best time to replace a roof in Queens

Most roofers here work from spring through fall, because shingles seal best above about 40°F. An autumn check of flashing, gutters and attic ventilation helps prevent winter ice dams.

Local tip: Many roofers here book up in late summer and fall, so get quotes early if you want the work done before winter.

Checking a roofer's license in New York

New York State doesn't license roofers statewide. Licensing is handled locally, and many counties and cities require home improvement contractors to be licensed, so check with your city or county. Our roofer checklist covers the rest.

Queens roofing questions

How much does a new roof cost in Queens?
Nationally, Angi data reported by NerdWallet puts the average near $9,500, with most jobs between about $5,800 and $46,000. Your price in Queens depends on size, pitch, material and local labor, so get a written quote.
Will insurance pay for roof damage in Queens?
Most homeowners policies cover sudden damage, such as a tree falling on the roof or a storm tearing off shingles, but not wear and tear or an old roof failing.
Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Queens?
Usually, yes. Your roofer should pull it with the Queens building department and show it to you before work starts.

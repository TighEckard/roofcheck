Common roof problems in California

  • Wildfire embers in dry seasons that can ignite roofs and gutters.
  • Intense sun and heat that dry out and crack shingles and underlayment.
  • Salt air near the water that corrodes metal flashing, fasteners and vents.

Class A fire-rated roofing such as tile, metal or rated asphalt shingles is common, and some areas require it. Ember-resistant vents and clean gutters help too.

Tip: Ask roofers about local fire-rating requirements and whether your area needs a permit for a reroof (most do).

Checking a roofer's license in California

California roofers must be licensed by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB), usually with a C-39 roofing classification. The CSLB website shows license status, bonds, workers' comp and any complaints.

Ask every contractor for their license or registration number and check it before you sign. Our roofer checklist covers the rest.

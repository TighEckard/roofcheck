Roof overlay vs. tear-off: can you put new shingles over old?

An overlay (also called a layover or re-roof) saves tear-off work, but it isn't always allowed or wise.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: An overlay puts new shingles over one existing layer, which skips tear-off labor and disposal. It's only an option when the old roof is flat, dry and sound, and when local code allows it. Most U.S. codes, based on the International Residential Code, don't allow a new layer over two existing layers. A full tear-off costs more but lets the roofer inspect and repair the decking, and it's what most manufacturers and roofers prefer.

When an overlay is a bad idea

  • The roof already has two layers.
  • The decking is soft, rotted or water-soaked, or the roof leaks in several places.
  • The old shingles are badly curled or buckled, so the new ones won't lie flat.
  • You live where ice dams form and need new ice-and-water barrier at the eaves.
  • You're filing an insurance claim. Many claims pay for removal of the damaged roof, so an overlay leaves money and inspection on the table.

Warranty and resale

Some shingle manufacturers limit or exclude warranty coverage for overlays, or offer their best warranties only on tear-offs. Read the specific warranty before you agree. Home buyers and inspectors may also ask how many layers a roof has. See roof warranties.

What to ask your roofer

  • How many layers are on the roof now?
  • Does our city or county allow an overlay here?
  • What does the shingle warranty say about overlays?
  • Can you quote both options so I can compare?

Frequently asked questions

How many layers of shingles are allowed?
Most U.S. codes based on the International Residential Code allow at most two layers, so you can't add a new layer over two existing ones. Your local building department has the final say.
Is an overlay cheaper?
Usually, because there's no tear-off labor or disposal. The savings can disappear if hidden deck damage causes problems later.
Can metal roofing go over shingles?
Sometimes, with the right underlayment or furring strips and if code allows it. Many roofers still recommend a tear-off so the deck can be checked.
Does an overlay void my warranty?
It depends on the manufacturer. Some reduce or exclude coverage for overlays. Read the warranty before you decide.

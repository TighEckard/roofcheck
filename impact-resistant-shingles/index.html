Are impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles worth it?

If you live where hail is common, Class 4 shingles are worth a close look. Here's what they are and how to decide.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: Impact-resistant shingles carry a Class 4 rating under the UL 2218 test, the highest impact rating for roofing. They usually cost more than standard architectural shingles, but in hail-prone states many insurers offer premium discounts that can offset part of the extra cost over time. They make the most sense in hail country, such as Texas, Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, Colorado and the Dakotas.

What "Class 4" means

Under UL 2218, steel balls of increasing size are dropped onto the roofing. Class 4 is the top rating: the product showed no cracks after impacts from a ball about 2 inches in diameter. Many Class 4 asphalt shingles use a rubberized (SBS-modified) asphalt that's more flexible than standard shingles. Metal roofing can also earn a Class 4 rating, although it may still dent.

Pros

  • Fewer hail claims and less chance of a full replacement after a moderate hailstorm.
  • Possible insurance discounts in hail-prone states.
  • Many carry stronger wind ratings too.

Cons

  • Higher up-front cost than standard shingles.
  • Not hail-proof. Very large hail can still damage them.
  • Discounts vary by insurer and aren't guaranteed.

How to decide

  1. Check how often hail hits your area. Our city pages flag hail where it's a main roof risk.
  2. Ask your insurance agent what discount, if any, applies to a Class 4 roof and what proof they need.
  3. Get quotes for both standard and Class 4 shingles so you can compare the real price difference.
  4. Keep the product label or invoice showing the UL 2218 Class 4 rating.

Frequently asked questions

What does Class 4 mean on shingles?
Class 4 is the highest rating in the UL 2218 impact test, in which steel balls are dropped on the shingle. A Class 4 shingle showed no cracking when hit by the largest ball, about 2 inches across.
Do impact-resistant shingles lower insurance premiums?
Often, in hail-prone states. Many insurers offer a discount for Class 4 roofs, and the size varies widely. Ask your agent before you choose a shingle, and keep the product paperwork.
Are impact-resistant shingles hail-proof?
No. They resist damage much better than standard shingles, but very large hail can still damage them.

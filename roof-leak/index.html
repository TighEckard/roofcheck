Roof leaking? What to do right now

A drip from the ceiling is stressful. Here's how to limit the damage today and get the leak fixed for good.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Right now: limit the damage

  1. Move furniture and electronics out of the way and put a bucket under the drip.
  2. If a ceiling stain is bulging, water may be pooling above it. Carefully poke a small hole in the center to let it drain into a bucket, which can keep the ceiling from collapsing.
  3. If water is near light fixtures or outlets, turn off power to that area at the breaker if you can do so safely.
  4. Take photos and video of the leak and any damage.

Common causes of roof leaks

  • Flashing around chimneys, vents, skylights and walls that has cracked, rusted or pulled away.
  • Missing or damaged shingles after wind or hail.
  • Pipe boots (the rubber seals around plumbing vents) that have cracked with age.
  • Ice dams in cold climates that push water under shingles.
  • Clogged gutters that back water up under the roof edge.
  • An aging roof where the shingles and underlayment have simply worn out.

Get it inspected

Water often travels along rafters before it drips, so the leak on your ceiling may be several feet from the hole in the roof. A roofer can trace it and tell you whether a repair will do or whether the roof is near the end of its life. See repair or replace?

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a tarp on my roof myself?
Only if it's safe. Wet and steep roofs are dangerous. Many roofers offer emergency tarping, and your insurer may expect reasonable steps to prevent more damage.
Will homeowners insurance pay for a roof leak?
It depends on the cause. Sudden damage, like a tree limb or wind, is often covered, while leaks from age or wear usually aren't. Your policy and insurer decide.

How to file a roof insurance claim, step by step

A roof claim goes more smoothly when you know the steps and keep good records. Here's the process from storm to final payment.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

This is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Your policy and your insurer decide what's covered.

Quick answer: To file a roof claim, document the damage with dated photos, prevent further damage with a tarp if it's safe, read your policy's deductible and deadlines, report the claim promptly, meet the insurance adjuster with your own roofer if possible, and compare the insurer's estimate to a written roofer's estimate before approving work.

Step 1: Make sure it's safe, then document everything

Stay off a wet or damaged roof. From the ground, photograph missing shingles, dented gutters and vents, debris and interior water stains. Note the date of the storm. Local news or weather service reports of hail or high winds that day are useful to keep.

Step 2: Prevent more damage

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to stop further damage, like tarping an opening or catching drips. Keep receipts. Your insurer may reimburse reasonable temporary repairs.

Step 3: Read your policy

  • Deductible: Is it a flat amount or a percentage of your home's insured value? Many hurricane and wind-hail deductibles are percentages.
  • Roof payment terms: Does the policy pay replacement cost (RCV) or actual cash value (ACV) for the roof? Some policies use a payment schedule based on roof age.
  • Deadlines: Note how long you have to report damage and to complete repairs.

Step 4: Get a professional inspection

A roofer can tell you whether the damage is storm-related and whether repairs are likely to exceed your deductible. Get the findings in writing with photos. Be wary of anyone who pressures you to sign a contract before your claim is decided. See roofing scams and red flags.

Step 5: File the claim

Report it by phone, app or website. Give the date of loss, describe the damage and share photos. Write down your claim number and the adjuster's name.

Step 6: The adjuster's inspection

The insurer sends an adjuster to inspect the roof. It often helps to have your roofer there to point out damage. Ask for a copy of the adjuster's estimate.

Step 7: Compare estimates and resolve differences

Compare the insurer's estimate line by line with your roofer's. Missing items often include drip edge, starter shingles, ice-and-water barrier, permits and code-required upgrades. If something is missing, send documentation and ask for a supplement or re-inspection. If you still disagree, check your policy's appraisal clause or contact your state's department of insurance.

Step 8: Get the work done and collect the rest

With replacement-cost coverage, insurers often pay the depreciated amount first and release the rest (the "recoverable depreciation") after you send proof the work is complete. Keep the final invoice, permit and photos.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting months to report damage.
  • Signing over your claim rights without reading what you signed.
  • Accepting an offer to waive your deductible, which is fraud.
  • Paying a large deposit before the claim is approved.

Frequently asked questions

Should I call my insurer or a roofer first?
For obvious damage, report it to your insurer promptly, since policies set deadlines. Many homeowners also have a roofer inspect first so they know whether damage is worth a claim, especially when it might be close to the deductible.
What is the difference between ACV and RCV?
Actual cash value (ACV) pays what the roof was worth at the time of the loss, after depreciation. Replacement cost value (RCV) pays to replace it with a similar new roof, often in two payments: the depreciated amount first and the rest after the work is done.
What if I disagree with the adjuster?
Ask for the estimate in writing, send your roofer's estimate and photos, and ask for a re-inspection. Many policies include an appraisal clause for disputes over the amount, and you can contact your state's department of insurance.
Will a roof claim raise my premium?
It can. Insurers price policies using claim history and local risk. A weather claim affecting a whole area is generally treated differently from a one-off claim, but rules vary by state and insurer.

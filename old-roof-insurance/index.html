Old roof and home insurance: what to know

Insurers pay close attention to roof age. Here's how it can affect your policy and your options.

Updated November 18, 2026 · By the Bedrock Roof team

Quick answer: As a roof ages, many insurers charge more, switch roof coverage from replacement cost to actual cash value (which deducts for depreciation), require an inspection, or decline to renew the policy. Rules vary by insurer and state. If you get a notice about your roof, ask exactly what the insurer needs, get an independent inspection, and compare other insurers before replacing a roof that still has useful life.

What insurers may do

  • Raise premiums or deductibles for older roofs.
  • Pay actual cash value (ACV) for the roof instead of replacement cost (RCV). See ACV vs RCV.
  • Require a roof inspection or a roof certification.
  • Non-renew the policy. Many states require advance written notice.

What you can do

  1. Read the notice carefully and ask what would satisfy the insurer.
  2. Get a roof inspection with photos and an estimate of remaining life.
  3. Ask an independent agent to shop other carriers.
  4. If you replace the roof, send the permit and invoice to your insurer, and ask about discounts.
  5. Contact your state insurance department if you think a notice doesn't follow state rules.

Frequently asked questions

Can my insurance company drop me because of my roof's age?
In many states, insurers can decline to renew based on roof age or condition, usually with advance notice. Some states have added limits. Check your state insurance department's rules.
What age roof do insurers consider old?
It varies by insurer and material. Asphalt shingle roofs past roughly 15 to 20 years often get extra scrutiny.
Will insurance pay for a new roof if mine is old?
Only if a covered event, like hail or wind, caused damage. Wear and age aren't covered, and older roofs may be paid at actual cash value.
Does a roof certification help?
It can. A certification or inspection report showing remaining life may satisfy some insurers or buyers.
